Map the office before comparing products

Walk the office at a normal busy hour and mark the places where people look up most often. The useful clues are usually ordinary: a walkway behind a monitor, a printer path beside a finance desk, or a reception chair with a clear view of paperwork. Those observations tell you where a divider can actually improve the day.

Choose height by sightline

Height should be decided from a seated position, not from a product photo. Sit at the desk, look toward the distraction path, and measure what has to be blocked. A lower panel may be enough to hide keyboard movement, while a taller panel may be needed beside a busy corridor.

Compare material tradeoffs

Fabric and PET felt feel warm and can soften the mood of a workstation row, but they need sensible cleaning expectations. Acrylic and polycarbonate keep light moving and wipe quickly, but they may show fingerprints and glare. Laminate or framed panels can suit heavy-use offices where edges take daily abuse.

Pilot the rollout

Start with a small installation across different desk types: one bench run, one reception-adjacent station, one high-focus role, and one flexible desk. Let people work with the panels for several days before judging them. First impressions often miss cable conflicts and chair movement issues.
