Start with the bench system

Shared bench layouts are efficient, but they concentrate equipment and movement. Before choosing a divider, study the bench spine: power rail, cable trough, monitor arms, modesty panel, and the way paired workers face each other. The divider has to cooperate with that system.

Face-to-face versus side-by-side

Face-to-face benches often need a central screen that reduces direct eye contact and monitor visibility. Side-by-side benches may benefit more from aisle-facing side panels or end screens that block traffic. The direction of distraction decides the divider position.

Cables, monitors, and desk depth

Cable access is the hidden test of bench dividers. Check whether the panel blocks grommets, under-desk trays, monitor-arm clamps, or power modules. If someone has to remove the divider to plug in a laptop dock, the setup will not last.

Bench layout rehearsal

Tape the proposed panel footprint onto one bench before ordering. Pull chairs back, swing monitor arms, plug in laptop docks, and walk the aisle with two people passing. This quick rehearsal reveals whether the divider respects the bench system or fights it.

If the bench run serves different roles, label the needs by seat. One spot may need an aisle shield, another may need a face-to-face screen, and another may need no panel at all. Bulk consistency is useful, but bench layouts still reward seat-by-seat judgment.
