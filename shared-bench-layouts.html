Desk Dividers for Shared Bench Layouts

Planning notes for benching runs, cable clearance, and team movement when choosing open office desk dividers.

If your team is comparing 7 Best Desk Dividers for Open Offices, this page looks specifically at desk dividers for shared bench layouts. The goal is to make the buying decision more practical: measure the space, understand the behavior around the desk, and choose a divider that improves the workday without creating a new obstacle.

Begin with the bench spine

Shared bench desks are efficient, but they concentrate movement, elbows, cables, and screens. A divider plan should respect the spine of the bench: power rails, cable troughs, monitor arms, and the shared center line. If the panel fights that spine, the installation will feel messy from day one.

Balance pair privacy

Two people facing each other need different things from two people sitting side by side. Face-to-face benches often benefit from central panels that block direct eye contact, while side-by-side runs may need end caps or aisle-facing screens. Match the panel to the direction of distraction.
